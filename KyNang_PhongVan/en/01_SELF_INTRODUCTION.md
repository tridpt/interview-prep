# 1. Self-introduction ("Tell me about yourself")

This is almost **always the first question**. A good answer sets the tone for the whole interview. Don't give a long biography — "sell" yourself in 60–90 seconds.

## The "Present → Past → Future" formula

1. **Present**: who you are, what you do/study (1 sentence).
2. **Past**: 1–2 achievements/projects most relevant to the role (emphasize skills + results).
3. **Future**: why you're excited about this role/company.

## Template (fresher/junior dev)

> "I'm [name], a recent grad / final-year CS student focused on web development. Recently I built several hands-on projects: for example **a 2FA (TOTP) manager** where I **implemented the algorithm myself per RFC 6238** and encrypted data locally with scrypt + AES, verified by official test vectors; and **a food-map web app** using Leaflet with two-tier localStorage + IndexedDB storage, 47 Playwright tests and CI/CD. Through these I got comfortable *owning both backend and frontend*, writing tests, and thinking about security/UX. I'm looking for a [frontend/backend/fullstack] role to learn in an environment with a real product and solid processes."

## Tips

- **Match the JD**: pick 1–2 projects whose tech overlaps the job description.
- **One "why" closer**: show motivation, not just a list.
- **Practice out loud, timed**: aim for ~75 seconds. Too long rambles.
- **Don't read your CV**: tell it as a story with a peak (an achievement).

## 30-second version (when asked to "keep it short")

> "I'm a full-stack-leaning web dev: I've built projects with both a backend (FastAPI/Flask Python) and a frontend (vanilla JS/PWA), focusing on automated tests and security. I want to apply that to a real product at your company."

## Traps to avoid
- Long personal life details (hometown, unrelated hobbies).
- Vague claims ("I'm hardworking, eager to learn") with no evidence.
- Claiming tech you've never used — it shows when probed.

> 💡 Prepare **two versions**: a 30-second and a 90-second one. The interviewer will signal which they want.
