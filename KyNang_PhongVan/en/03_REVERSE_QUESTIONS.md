# 3. Questions to ask the interviewer

At the end there's almost always "Do you have any questions for us?". **Don't say "No".** Asking back shows initiative and genuine interest, and helps you evaluate the company. Prepare 4–6.

## About the work & tech
- What does a typical day in this role look like?
- What stack does the team use, and is there technical debt you're prioritizing?
- What's the team's code-review and merge process?
- What's the team's **CI/CD and test coverage** like? (great when you've built test-heavy projects like quan-quen-map/totp-manager)
- When there's a production incident, how does the team handle it and run postmortems?

## About growth
- Where does a fresher/junior typically progress to after 6–12 months here?
- Is there a mentor / pair-programming setup for newcomers?
- How does the company support learning (courses, materials, learning time)?

## About the product & team
- How many users / what scale does the product have?
- What keeps you engaged with this team/company?
- What's the biggest challenge the team faces right now?

## "Point-scoring" questions matching your profile
If you built the projects in this pack, ask questions that show you've thought deeply:
- "I tend to focus on **reducing false positives** in automated tools — does the team have a quality-evaluation process for automated features?"
- "I've built an **offline-first/PWA** app — does your product need offline operation or data sync?"
- "I care about **securing sensitive data** (encryption-at-rest, KDFs) — how does the team handle sensitive user data?"

## Avoid
- Asking about salary/leave in the first question (save for the offer stage unless they bring it up).
- Questions the website/JD already answers (shows you didn't research).
- Curt yes/no questions — prefer open ones that get them talking.

> 💡 Pick 2 questions **based on what the interviewer just said** during the session — a signal of active listening, more impressive than memorized questions.
